Why UTVs Need Limit Straps More Than Any Other Vehicle UTVs are purpose-built for aggressive off-road use — rock crawling, sand dunes, tight trails, and high-speed desert running.

Factory UTV suspensions are designed with a specific droop range in mind, and that range is calibrated to match the length of the CV axles, brake hose lengths, and shock travel. When you add a lift, run extended-travel shocks, or push the suspension harder than stock, you quickly exceed what the factory components can handle at full droop. Unlike trucks and Jeeps where full droop typically happens slowly during trail crawling, UTVs frequently achieve full droop in mid-air — a jump landing, a ledge drop, or a high-speed whoop section. When a UTV lands with a wheel hanging, the suspension slams to full extension in milliseconds.

Without limit straps, that impact is transferred directly to the CV axles. A single hard landing can rip a CV joint if the suspension is free to over-extend. This is why you see limit straps on virtually every professionally built UTV race vehicle.
