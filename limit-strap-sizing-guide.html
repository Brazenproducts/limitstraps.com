Limit Strap Sizing Guide

How to measure, length charts by vehicle, and material comparison

Home › Sizing Guide

Getting the right limit strap length is critical — too short and you limit your suspension travel prematurely; too long and the strap won't engage before something breaks. This guide walks you through how to measure correctly and use our length charts to order the right straps the first time.

How to Measure

You'll need a tape measure, a jack, and jack stands. Here's the step-by-step:

  1. Support the vehicle on jack stands with the suspension hanging at full droop (wheels hanging down as far as they'll go)
  2. Identify your two mounting points: one on the frame, one on the axle or control arm
  3. Measure the straight-line distance between the center of each mounting hole
  4. Measure at full droop, then order straps 1-2" shorter than that measurement. Nylon webbing stretches approximately 1" per foot under load, so the straps will reach full extension naturally. The nylon stretches approximately 1" per foot under load, so the straps will reach full extension naturally.
  5. Order straps at that length, or round to the nearest half-inch — these straps can be ordered to any custom length

💡 Tip

Measure at full droop, not at rest. Your suspension geometry changes dramatically through its travel, and you want to size for the worst-case (maximum droop) position.

End Configurations

Sewn loops: Most common. One or both ends have a sewn loop that accepts a bolt. Simple and reliable.

Clevis ends: Fork-style ends that clip into a receiver. Quick removal option.

Bolt-through: Flat end designed to bolt directly between two existing mount points.

Our Recommendation
