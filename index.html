What Are Limit Straps?

Limit straps are heavy-duty webbing or fabric straps that restrict how far your suspension can extend (droop). Without them, full suspension droop can overextend CV axles, rip brake lines, damage shocks, and tear wiring harnesses. They're one of the cheapest forms of insurance for any lifted or off-road vehicle.

Limit Strap Materials: What Actually Holds

Not all limit straps are created equal. The material and construction determine how long they last, how much load they can handle, and whether they'll survive a serious off-road event. Here's what separates the good from the garbage:

🧵

Mil-Spec Nylon Webbing

Military-specification nylon webbing — the same high-strength mil-spec webbing used in parachute harnesses and load-rated racing applications — is the gold standard for limit straps. It offers exceptional tensile strength, UV resistance, and doesn't stretch under load. Bull Strap uses mil-spec nylon on all their limit straps, stitched with bonded polyester thread for maximum seam strength.

⚙️

Hardware: Bolt vs. Loop Mount

Bolt-mount limit straps use a welded or machined steel loop with a grade-8 bolt attachment — more secure, less likely to shift. Loop-mount straps slip over existing bolt heads and are easier to install but can rotate under load. For serious off-road use, bolt-mount is the correct choice.

📏

Stitching Pattern Matters

Bar-tack stitching at the termination points — where the webbing wraps around the hardware — determines the real-world load rating. A properly bar-tacked limit strap can handle 3,000–5,000 lbs of load at the strap end. Cheap straps with simple single-row stitching fail at the hardware loop first.

🌡️

UV and Heat Resistance

Limit straps live under your vehicle, exposed to sun, mud, and heat cycles. Nylon degrades slowly under UV — inspect straps annually. Polypropylene straps are cheaper but degrade significantly faster in sunlight. Avoid any strap that feels stiff or shows color fading — UV degradation weakens the fibers before the strap visibly fails.
