Best Limit Straps for Jeep Wrangler

JK, JL, and TJ specific fitment — protect your axles on the trail

Home › Best Limit Straps › Jeep Wrangler

Jeep Wranglers are the most commonly modified off-road vehicles in America, and limit straps are one of the most important — yet overlooked — upgrades for any lifted Jeep. Once you add 2.5" or more of lift, your suspension can droop far enough to damage CV axles, stretch brake lines, and over-extend shocks. Limit straps solve this problem for under $150.

Why Jeeps Need Limit Straps

The Wrangler's solid axle design allows massive articulation — which is great for crawling, but dangerous for components when the suspension fully extends. Here's what happens without limit straps on a lifted Jeep:

  • CV axle over-extension — The #1 trail killer. Replacement cost: $400–$800 per axle
  • Brake line stretching — Can lead to brake failure on the trail. Extremely dangerous.
  • Shock damage — Shocks bottoming out on extension destroys internal valving
  • Sway bar link failure — Disconnected or not, links can bend or snap at full droop

Jeep Limit Strap Fitment by Model

Jeep Wrangler JL (2018–Present)

The JL's independent front suspension (on 4-door Rubicon) and revised rear geometry mean limit strap placement differs from the JK. Most JL builds with 2.5"+ lift benefit from rear limit straps mounted between the frame and axle tube. Front straps are recommended for 3.5"+ lifts.

Jeep Wrangler JK (2007–2018)

The JK is the most commonly modified Wrangler generation. Its solid front and rear axles allow huge articulation, making limit straps critical for any lift over 2.5". The JK's longer wheelbase (4-door) requires slightly different strap lengths than the 2-door.

Jeep Wrangler TJ (1997–2006)

The TJ's coil-spring suspension (replacing the YJ's leaf springs) introduced more droop travel, making limit straps more important. TJ builds with 3"+ lift should absolutely run limit straps, especially on the rear where droop travel is greatest.
